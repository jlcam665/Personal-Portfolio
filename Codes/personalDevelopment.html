MY FAVOURITE SCENE

"Our deepest fear is not that we are inadequate. Our deepest fear is that we are powerful beyond measure. We ask ourselves, who am I to be brilliant, gorgeous, talented, and fabulous? Actually, who are you not to be? We were born to make manifest the glory of God that is within us. And as we let our own light shine, we unconsciously give other people permission to do the same."

"Does that mean anything to you?"


This scene from the 2006 film, Akeelah and the Bee, holds a special place in my heart. Before this scene, there was a training montage where Akeelah was introduced to words with many origins, such as Latin. Dr. Larabee's goal was to build her confidence, helping her break down the words in such a way that they no longer seem intimidating. There was still a block in Akeelah's mind. She thought she would not be able to make it to the national spelling bee, whether it be because of her background, her competition, or people around her putting doubts in her head. With this quote, Dr. Larabee was given room to establish the seriousness of her training and tactfully explain she needs to approach the competition with a winner's attitude.

Ever since I first saw that scene, from single digits to now, it inspires me to put my best foot forward and believe I am worthy of being where I am. Because who am I not to be?


RICHEST MAN IN BABYLON

The book entitled, "Richest Man in Babylon offers practical guidance in the world of financial responsibility. The advice it offers is simple, but in that simplicity comes great; timeless value. The two key chapters within the book that offer this advice are, "Seven Cures for a Lean Purse," and, "Five Laws of Gold." To fully benefit from the advice given, an analysis of each chapter will be done along with personal reflection.

The book highlights that the prosperity of a nation is dependent on the financial prosperity of its citizens. This is where the king of Babylon, Sargon, discovers an issue. The spending power of the people was greatly reduced because a great deal of wealth had quickly amassed amongst a few men in the city. The people lacked knowledge on how to maintain and increase their capital, which is when the king brought in Arkad, one of the rich men. Arkad began to pass on knowledge to the people in the form of several principles that relate to money management.

"If thou select one of thy baskets and put into it each morning ten eggs and take out from it each evening nine eggs, what will eventually happen?" "It will become in time overflowing."

The principle that can be gleaned from this is that we can save a portion of our earnings. The benefit of this is that the savings in time will grow. He also highlighted the phenomenon where necessary expenses increase alongside wages. In such cases, it is encouraged we protest such action. This is reflected in the world currently, where it seems our month-end paychecks can barely survive bills and/or groceries. With that, he emphasizes that our necessary expenses are not the same as our desires, meaning one does not satisfy the other. Of course, we should not try to satisfy every desire, but some. The ultimate lesson in this regard is to budget our money, using one-tenth for spending. Additionally, Arkad points out that while our purse may be fattening, it is not labouring. It is not earning more money without our input. In that case, Arkad relates a story of how he loaned money to a shield-maker, who returned his funds with interest. The lesson from this is that we can invest a portion of our earnings. Further he warns that we should avoid risky investments, and not spend more than our earnings from our investments. Gaining knowledge from experienced investors is also valuable. Lastly he encouraged his subjects to secure a portion of their funds, not for present enjoyment, but enjoyment in our later years. Overall, this chapter taught me that no matter which position I find myself in financially, I have the chance to gain funds if I also have the ability to work. While working, my income can increase by setting aside portions of my funds for saving and necessary expenses. If I maintain that discipline, I can enjoy the spoils of my fortune into my elderly years.

The Five Laws of Gold

The subjects are given two options. Whether to receive a bag of gold, or a tablet engraved with wisdom. Many of the subjects chose the gold, but were corrected. For without the wisdom, they were described to be as starving wolves who were tossed some food. The wolves would surely fight over it, devouring every morsel. Then when the next day approaches, they would gladly do the same again. The wisdom would help preserve the treasure for a longer time. The wisdom imparted here is from Arkad's father in the form of five laws.

Namely:

  1. Gold cometh gladly and in increasing quantity to any man who will put by not less than one-tenth or his earnings to create an estate for his future and that for his family.
  2. Gold laboureth diligently and contentedly for the wise owner who finds for it profitable employment, multiplying even as the flocks of the field.
  3. Gold clingeth to the protection of the cautious owner who invests it under the advice of men wise in its handling.
  4. Gold slippeth away from the man who invests it in businesses or purposes with which he is not familiar or which are not approved by those skilled in its keep.
  5. Gold flees the man who would force it to impossible earnings or who followeth the alluring advice of tricksters and schemers or who trusts it to his own inexperience and romantic desires in investment.

These five laws are very similar to the principles expressed in the previous chapter. They fundamentally emphasize the need for investing a portion of our earnings. They also speak to being cautious of which ventures we invest in, as some will cause us to lose our money much quicker than we earn it. Lastly we are warned not to rely purely on our own judgement, especially with our inexperience when making investment decisions. In conclusion, The Richest Man in Babylon offers simple, but timeless truths on money management. These truths not only help us to manage whatever fortunes we may have, but build upon them. We can build upon them by saving portions of our income and through investments in certain businesses or ventures. Of course, we must be careful not to spend more than we invest, nor must we pool our funds into risky, scam-like ventures. As long as we are willing to apply discipline to our work and investment ventures however, we will be able to increase our earnings for our needs and wants now, and in the future.
