LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch


The proliferation of Large language Models (LLMs) in a rapidly evolving digital world is both fascinating and existential to many. With its increased integration in many aspects of everyday life, questions regarding its utility, both in the positive and negative direction arise. Should the output of an LLM be the final output used? Can LLMs replace human cognition? There are however several reasons as to why LLMs should be viewed more as a tool. LLM hallucination is possible. Cognitive offloading towards LLMs can carry many benefits but also many risks (Ye & Guo, 2026).

Compared to a traditional web search, LLMs can handle large, detailed queries requiring a specific response (Hofman et al, 2025). The disadvantage with this method of querying however is that LLMs at times may hallucinate. LLM hallucination is referred to as a phenomenon where an LLM deviates from its training data, giving a user a seemingly plausible but flawed response to their prompt (Bang et al, 2025). Additionally, compared to traditional search, the sources used by the LLM may not be readily exposed to the user for easy cross-referencing or verification. Furthermore, sources cited by the LLM may be different from the information it presents. This means that for research or any query, the result of the LLM should be scrutinized or refined to ensure correct results.

This however can develop cognitive atrophy. In relation to that is the term cognitive offloading. This is defined as the act of relegating certain activities to external entities (Guo & Ye, 2026). For example, a student might rely on a calculator to help solve a lengthy math question. This can greatly enhance efficiency, but also lead to weaker decision-making. From this, it can be concluded that delegating critical thinking purely to LLMs can lead to cognitive atrophy. While cognitive offloading is not inherently harmful, it is best to balance the use of LLMs with human decision-making.

In conclusion, the integration of LLMs into certain miscellaneous and administrative activities has made the act of doing them more efficient. But while LLMs show a great number of capabilities, there are downsides to offloading all parts of the critical thinking process to them. Therefore it is best to use LLMs as a tool to assist in completing those tasks rather than a crutch, which determines the final output.
